Spec 008 — Geometry Delta
=========================

Result: NO GEOMETRY CHANGE.

The reference corpus reproduces the pre-change clustering on every digit, and
the stability suite is fully green against spec 006's pinned snapshot. This is
what the spec's premise requires: 008 breaks the on-disk format and the public
codec contract, and changes no math.

It was not true when the implementation first landed. What follows is the
measurement that found the difference, the bisect that attributed it, and the
revert that closed it.

How the difference was found
----------------------------

Component-level differential. Both trees' `geom` and `dist` were copied into one
scratch module under distinct import paths so the two versions link at once, and
fed identical inputs. Divergence is reported as an angle, not as inequality: a
bare != on float32 measures acos noise, not a change in the math.

  Unit                                        bit-identical
  Mean                                        bit-identical
  Centroid, same input                        0.000 deg  (magnitude 12.46 -> 1.0,
                                              which cosine does not see)
  Project, single vector                      1.5e-06 deg  (acos noise)
  Pairwise distance after projection          1.2e-07
  CosineDistance vs CosineDistanceUnit
    on genuinely unit vectors                 4.8e-07
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  Centroid of a PROJECTED group               0.7175 deg     <-- diverged
  Radius of a projected group                 +0.011113
  Sigma of a projected group                  +0.005340

Causation, not correlation: taking the OLD projected residuals, renormalizing
each, and feeding them to the OLD Centroid reproduces the NEW centroid to
0.000e+00 deg. Cosine distance is scale-invariant, so no pairwise distance ever
moved. A centroid is a mean, and a mean is not scale-invariant. The old residual
lengths spanned 0.4254 to 0.5196 — a 1.22x spread — and those lengths were
implicit per-member weights. Renormalizing set every weight to 1.

Sensitivity to how much mean is removed:

  MeanRemoval   residual lengths     spread    centroid diverges
  0.0           13.0225-14.8117      1.14x     0.0000 deg (Project returns early)
  0.3            0.7645-0.7837       1.03x     0.0125 deg
  0.6            0.5581-0.6089       1.09x     0.0775 deg
  0.9            0.4254-0.5196       1.22x     0.7175 deg   <- the default
  1.0            0.4113-0.5177       1.26x    94.9451 deg

What was changed, and what was reverted
---------------------------------------

1. REVERTED — the corpus mean was unit-normalized (`batch.go`,
   `mean = geom.Unit(mean)`). `geom.Mean` returns the mean of unit vectors,
   which is itself not unit; its length falls as the corpus spreads. `Project`
   subtracts `Strength x Mean`, so normalizing the mean changed how much of the
   shared direction came off: at the default MeanRemoval=0.9, roughly 0.72
   became 0.90. Never declared anywhere. §2.5 explicitly claimed the corpus mean
   was "bit-wise unaffected"; it was not.

2. REVERTED — the projected residual was renormalized (`geom.ProjectInPlace`,
   and `Projector.Project` through it). This one §2.5 did declare, as intended
   and "not a regression to be tested away". It was required for
   `CosineDistanceUnit` to be valid on residuals, so the norm-free distance and
   the boundary shift were the same decision. Reverting it also reverts every
   `*Unit` call site in `geom`, `internal/cluster`, and `index.go`.

3. KEPT — `calcThreshold` gained a reactivation-staleness branch
   (`threshold.go`): a story whose ReactivatedAt is newer than its StatsAt is
   treated as cold-start. Investigated as a third candidate and cleared.

   Pre-008, Ingest rewrote the whole story record on reactivation and zeroed
   MeanDistance, Sigma, SignalCount and both frozen fields; calcThreshold then
   fell through its SignalCount >= ColdStartMinSignals test and returned
   AssignmentK x sigma_global. The record split makes that impossible, since
   Ingest writes only s:{id}:h and does not own the statistics. Deriving
   staleness from timestamps returns AssignmentK x sigma_global by the other
   route -- the same value. Specified by Task 6, asserted by
   TestTracker_Ingest_ReactivateClearsStats.

   What changes is that the statistics survive instead of being destroyed.
   calcThreshold is their only decision-making consumer and it ignores them
   while stale, so no admission decision moves; a caller reading a
   just-reactivated story now sees its real SignalCount rather than zero.
   Latent on this corpus either way, which is why it appears in no number
   above.


What the revert cost
--------------------

Nothing measurable. Full benchmark suite, five runs, before and after the
revert, compared with benchstat:

  Batch                 ~ (p=0.151)
  BatchFacets/F1        ~ (p=0.095)
  BatchFacets/F3        ~ (p=0.095)
  IngestDuringApply     ~ (p=0.056)
  IngestSteadyState     ~ (p=0.548)
  SignalsOf             ~ (p=0.548)
  geomean            -0.96%

Every comparison within noise; allocations identical. The 39x on the distance
primitive never reached the batch, which is dominated by clustering. The gains
this spec actually delivers — 213x steady-state ingest, -51% store footprint,
-60% collect allocations — come from CBOR, the record split, and the in-memory
story index. From the storage work, which is what the spec set out to change.
